Diameter and implications of a neighbourhood relation

Small diameter of the relation

The diameter of a relation is defined as the longest distance between any two elements in the corresponding graph of that relation.

The diameter of the neighbourhood relation should be upper-bounded by a polynomial function of the task size.

This property guarantees that the total number of iterations in the local search algorithm is acceptable. More precisely, its time complexity is bounded by a polynomial.

Example: For the Knapsack problem, give one positive and one negative example of a neighbourhood relation.

Implications of the properties

The four properties guarantee:

- The best solution can be reached because of connectedness.
- The local search algorithm has polynomial time complexity because of the other properties.
