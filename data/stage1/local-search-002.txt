Binary relations and neighbourhoods

Graph of a binary relation

Given a binary relation R ⊆ V × V on the set V, its corresponding directed graph G = (V, E) is defined as follows:

- The set of vertices is V.
- The set of edges E is exactly the set of all pairs v, v' ∈ V such that v is in relation R with v', meaning that (v, v') ∈ R.

Neighbourhood

For each current solution s, the set of solutions available in one iteration move from s to one of its neighbours is called the neighbourhood of s and is denoted N(s).

The neighbourhood is defined by a binary relation N ⊆ S × S on pairs of solutions, representing which pairs of solutions are neighbours. More precisely, s' is a neighbour of s if and only if (s, s') ∈ N.

Thus, the first step in approaching a problem with local search is to define the proper neighbourhood relation for that problem.
